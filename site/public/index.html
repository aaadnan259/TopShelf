The Engine

Three steps. Zero network requests.

Everything executes securely inside your browser tab without talking to external servers.

1

DOM Extraction

Under 4ms. TopShelf scans the rendered search page for ratings, review volume, price, "bought in past month" badges, and sponsored ribbons. Zero external API calls.

2

Bayesian Scoring

Laplace-smoothed confidence mathematics pulls thin-review products toward the category baseline until they earn statistical proof. Review farms lose their edge.

3

In-Place Reordering

Re-slots items into true quality rank without page reloads. Prime badges, image carousels, customer reviews, and cart buttons stay 100% operational.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Everything you need to know about how TopShelf works.

Does TopShelf collect or transmit my browsing data?
No. TopShelf has zero backend servers, zero analytics scripts, and zero network tracking permissions in its manifest. All DOM extraction, Bayesian mathematics, and slot reordering occur 100% locally on your device.
How does the Bayesian confidence formula work?
TopShelf uses empirical Bayes with Laplace volume smoothing: items with few reviews have their effective score pulled toward the category average (typically 4.2 stars). As verified volume grows, the item's true rating takes over. A 5.0 with 14 reviews cannot fake confidence against a 4.7 with 20,000 reviews.
Does TopShelf hide products completely?
By default, no products are deleted or hidden — they are simply reordered into honest quality order. You can toggle between Amazon's original order and TopShelf's ranked order at any time using the floating bottom-left pill.
Can I use TopShelf together with the Knockoff extension?
Yes! TopShelf and Knockoff work together. TopShelf detects Knockoff's brand verdicts on Amazon tiles: confirmed legitimate brands receive a trust boost, while flagged pseudo-brands receive appropriate ranking penalties.
Does this work on mobile or other browsers?
TopShelf is currently built for all Chromium-based desktop browsers including Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave, and Arc. Firefox and Safari versions are in active development.
